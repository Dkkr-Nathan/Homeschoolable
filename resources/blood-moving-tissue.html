Blood: The Moving Tissue

It looks like a red liquid, but blood is actually a living tissue full of specialized cells, proteins, chemical messages, and repair tools.

Blood, the “unknown organ”

More than a delivery fluid

Blood is classified as connective tissue because living cells are suspended in a liquid extracellular matrix called plasma. It transports oxygen and nutrients, removes wastes, carries hormones, regulates temperature and pH, fights infection, and repairs damaged vessels.

Meet the team

What is blood made of?

Think of blood as a busy moving city. Plasma is the roadway, and the formed elements are the workers travelling through it.

Plasma

The pale liquid portion of blood. It is mostly water but also carries proteins, electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, wastes, antibodies, and clotting proteins.

Red blood cells

Erythrocytes contain hemoglobin, the protein that binds oxygen. Their flexible disc shape helps them squeeze through tiny capillaries.

White blood cells

Leukocytes are immune cells. Different types detect threats, destroy microbes, coordinate inflammation, and remember previous infections.

Platelets

Thrombocytes are tiny cell fragments made from much larger bone-marrow cells. They rapidly gather at vessel injuries and help begin a clot.

Where blood comes from

The bone marrow blood-cell factory

Most blood cells begin as hematopoietic stem cells in red bone marrow. These stem cells can develop along different pathways to form red blood cells, several families of white blood cells, and platelet-producing megakaryocytes.

The clotting cascade

A controlled chain reaction

Hemostasis is the body’s process for stopping bleeding. The real clotting system is complex, but the big picture is manageable:

1. Vessel constriction

The injured vessel narrows, reducing blood flow through the damaged area.

2. Platelet plug

Platelets adhere to exposed vessel structures, activate, release signals, and recruit more platelets.

3. Coagulation

Clotting factors activate one another in a cascade that produces thrombin.

4. Fibrin reinforcement

Thrombin converts soluble fibrinogen into fibrin, creating a mesh that stabilizes the clot.

Important: clotting must be powerful enough to stop bleeding but controlled enough not to block healthy vessels. Natural anticoagulant systems and fibrinolysis help keep that balance.

Understanding check

A student says, “Platelets turn into a scab.” How would you correct that statement? Use at least two of these ideas: platelet plug, clotting factors, fibrin, blood vessel, skin.

Compare with a strong answer

Platelets help form an early plug inside the damaged blood vessel, but they do not simply become the scab. Clotting factors produce fibrin, which strengthens the clot. At the surface, the clot dries while the skin underneath begins repairing itself. The scab is therefore part of a larger blood-and-skin repair process.
