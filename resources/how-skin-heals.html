Health & biology

How does skin heal?

A small scrape starts a remarkable repair project. Cells communicate, defend, rebuild and strengthen the injured spot.

Your living shield

Skin has layers with different jobs

EpidermisOuter protective barrier
DermisBlood vessels, nerves, collagen, hair follicles and glands
Subcutaneous layerFat and connective tissue cushion the body
Why the epidermis matters

Its tightly packed cells help keep water in and many germs and irritants out. New epidermal cells help close a shallow wound.

Why deeper layers matter

The dermis supplies blood, sensation and strong collagen. Deeper injuries take longer to repair and are more likely to leave a scar.

The repair crew

Four overlapping stages

Healing is a team effort, and the stages overlap rather than waiting neatly in line.

1

Stop the leak

Blood vessels narrow and platelets help form a clot. The clot slows bleeding and creates a temporary seal.

2

Clean and defend

Immune cells remove damaged material and help protect the opening. Redness, warmth and mild swelling can be part of this response.

3

Rebuild

New skin cells move across the surface. New blood vessels grow, and collagen helps fill and support the repair.

4

Strengthen

Collagen is reorganized for weeks or months. A scar may flatten and fade, but repaired skin may not regain all its original strength.

Why good healing matters

Protect the repair zone

🧼

Clean hands and wound

For a minor cut or scrape, wash your hands, stop bleeding with gentle pressure and rinse the wound with cool running water.

🩹

Cover and protect

A clean bandage helps keep out dirt and prevents rubbing while the surface closes. Follow advice from a health professional for deeper wounds.

👀

Watch the trend

Normal healing should generally improve. Spreading redness, increasing pain, pus, fever or red streaks can signal infection.

Get medical help if bleeding will not stop, the wound is deep or gaping, something is stuck inside, it came from an animal or human bite, or infection may be developing. Serious or dirty wounds may also require a tetanus assessment.
