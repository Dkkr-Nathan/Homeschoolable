Sleep and the brain

Sleep may look quiet from the outside, but your brain is busy sorting memories, managing emotions, supporting growth and preparing you for tomorrow.

While you sleep

Your brain is still at work

Different systems use sleep to reset, organize and restore.

🧠

Learning

Sleep helps stabilize and organize new memories.

🧹

Cleanup

Brain activity and fluid movement change during sleep, supporting normal waste clearance.

🌱

Growth

Deep sleep supports physical restoration and normal growth processes.

🎭

Emotions

Enough sleep supports attention, mood and emotional regulation.

Your internal clock

Two forces shape sleep

Circadian rhythm is your approximately 24-hour timing system, strongly influenced by light and darkness. Sleep pressure gradually builds while you are awake and eases while you sleep. Together, they help determine when you feel alert or sleepy.

How much?

Growing brains need plenty of sleep

9–11hours each night for children ages 5–13
8–10hours each night for teens ages 14–17
Help your body clock

Small habits make sleep easier

Keep a steady schedule
Wake and sleep at similar times.
Find morning light
Daylight helps set the body clock.
Build a wind-down
Choose calm, repeatable activities before bed.
Move during the day
Regular activity supports healthy sleep.
Make the room sleep-friendly
Dark, quiet and comfortably cool works best.
Notice ongoing problems
Talk with a healthcare professional about persistent snoring, breathing pauses or daytime sleepiness.
